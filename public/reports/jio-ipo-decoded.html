03 · Why it's going public

Jio doesn't really need this money.

Almost all of it goes to paying off loans, which leaves Jio close to debt-free (apart from what it owes for airwaves). The bigger reason to list: a share price it can use as currency in future deals with big investors and partners.

06 · What the headlines missed

Two bills that haven't arrived yet.

Bill 01 · TaxFY26
Tax bill₹10,300 cr
Actually paid₹118 cr
While Jio is building its network, tax rules let it write off that spending faster, so most of the bill is pushed to later. Around FY29 that break runs out and Jio starts paying the full amount.
Bill 02 · AirwavesOwed to the government
₹0lakh crore
Now
2044
To run a mobile network, Jio buys the right to use airwaves (spectrum) from the government. It didn't pay upfront, so it pays in yearly instalments with about 7% interest until 2044.

Another way to see it

What you pay for ₹1 of what it owns

₹3for ₹1 of Jio
₹7.7for ₹1 of Airtel

Take everything a company owns and subtract what it owes. That's its net worth. Investors pay ₹3 for every ₹1 of Jio's net worth, and ₹7.7 for Airtel's, because each rupee inside Airtel earns about twice as much.
