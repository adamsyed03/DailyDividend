07 · The big question

Why would a Hollywood VFX studio make a ₹4,000 crore Indian film?

DD's read: Ramayana is a product, and at the same time an advert to Hollywood for what DNEG can do.

Dune: Part Two

DNEG was paid a fee for its work. The studios kept the $715 million box office.

Ramayana

Prime Focus paid for the film, so it keeps what the film earns.

Own the upside

Working only for fees is risky. Technicolor, the company behind The Lion King's visual effects, ran out of money and shut down in 2025.

Earn respect

Namit has said that until India makes films at Hollywood's scale, it will always be treated as second best.

A showroom

Every studio watching Ramayana is also watching DNEG and Brahma at work.

09 · The troubles

Three things that worried investors.

Why so much debt? It built up over years of buying and growing studios, losing money when Hollywood went on strike in 2023, and spending on new tech and its own films.

1
Court case

A lender took Prime Focus to court over a ₹354 crore loan and asked for it to be declared insolvent. Prime Focus paid ₹408 crore to settle, a few months before Ramayana came out.

2
Tax raid

In October 2026, income tax officers searched its offices and directors' homes for a week. So far, the company says it hasn't been asked to pay any extra tax.

3
Debt, the big one

It owes ₹5,001 crore more than the cash it holds, and the pile keeps growing.
