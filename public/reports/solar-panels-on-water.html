India is about to put 5,000 megawatts of solar panels on its lakes and dams.

₹5,070 crore approved by the government on 31 July 2026

A watchlist  ·  Seven companies

Read this first

Nobody has won anything yet.

Projects get handed out between now and 2031. A government body called SECI runs the process.

So this is not a list of winners. It is a list of companies that already do this kind of work.

Two words you need

Before any number makes sense.

Revenue
Money that came in last year.
Margin
Operating profit out of every ₹100 of sales.

A power plant and a factory will never look alike. Compare like with like.

The seven, in three groups

Every company here does one of three jobs.

Who owns the water NHPC · SJVN · NTPC Green
Who builds on it Tata Power · BHEL
Who makes the panels Waaree · Saatvik

Thirty seconds each. Then one line on what could go wrong.

Group one

Who owns the water

Government companies that already control India's reservoirs.

Three companies

01

NHPC

₹11,615 crRevenue
~48%Operating margin
  • India's biggest hydro company. Runs 28 power stations.
  • Its arm NHDC owns the reservoir under India's largest floating solar park.
  • Started a 50 MW floating plant in Kerala in March 2026. Over ₹260 crore.
Why it fits

You cannot float a panel without a lake. NHPC has the lakes.

The catch

Solar is still a small slice. This is a hydro company.

02

SJVN

₹4,528 crRevenue
Up 47%In a year
  • Government power company from Shimla. Hydro, solar, thermal.
  • Owns a 90 MW floating plant at Omkareshwar. Cost ₹646 crore.
  • Sells that power at ₹3.26 a unit on a 25 year contract.
Why it fits

The only one here that already knows what a floating plant earns.

The catch

Debt is up to ₹32,207 crore. Growth is being borrowed.

03

NTPC Green

₹3,035 crRevenue
~92%Operating margin
  • The green arm of NTPC, India's biggest power producer.
  • Went from 5,902 MW to 10,076 MW of clean power in a single year.
Why it fits

The NTPC group runs India's oldest floating plants.

The catch

Those plants sit in the parent company, not this one. This one is only now bidding for its first small floating project.

Group two

Who builds on it

The two companies that have actually done it.

Two companies

04

Tata Power

₹62,429 crRevenue
~25%Operating margin
  • Electricity end to end. Makes it, moves it, sells it to your house.
  • Built India's two largest floating plants. 126 MW at Omkareshwar, 101 MW in Kerala.
  • Omkareshwar was a ₹596 crore government contract. 213,460 panels. 26 months.
  • Clean energy is now ₹15,028 crore of sales, up 52% in a year.
Why it fits

The only one that can make the panel, build the plant and own it.

05

BHEL

₹33,782 crRevenue
Thin margins, huge order bookThe shape of it
  • The old government engineering giant. Builds power plants for everyone else.
  • Built India's first big floating plant. 100 MW at Ramagundam, for ₹423 crore.
  • Order book of ₹2.2 lakh crore. Roughly seven years of work already booked.
Why it fits

When the government needs something hard built on water, this is the default.

The catch

About ₹7 of operating profit per ₹100 of sales. Volume, not margin.

Group three

Who makes the panels

Floating or not, someone has to build the actual panel.

Two companies

06

Waaree Energies

₹26,537 crRevenue
22%Operating margin
  • The biggest solar panel maker in India. And the biggest outside China, anywhere.
  • Sales almost doubled last year. Order book of ₹53,000 crore.
  • Also building a battery factory. This scheme makes batteries compulsory.
Why it fits

5,000 MW of panels have to come from somewhere.

The catch

Money is going out faster than it comes in. Watch the cash.

07

Saatvik Green

₹4,548 crRevenue
~13%Operating margin
  • Smaller panel maker. Listed on the market only last year.
  • Sales more than doubled. Order book of about ₹8,000 crore.
  • Says it has supplied panels to floating projects before, and expects this scheme to drive growth.
Why it fits

Panels, construction and batteries in one place. Exactly what this scheme pays for.

The catch

Smallest and newest here. Less history to judge it on.

The one thing to remember

The money only arrives at the end.

The government pays ₹1 crore per megawatt. But only after the plant is switched on and working.

This is not a subsidy for promising. It is a payment for finishing. So watch who actually finishes.
